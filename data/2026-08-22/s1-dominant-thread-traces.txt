S1 dominant-thread collection -- OnePlus 13, 2026-08-22
Observer: tools/dominant-thread-observer.sh, 250 ms / top 10, validated in
data/2026-08-22/s1-observer-validation.txt. Median window 260 ms in every trace.

Two module states:
  ON   = op13perf level 2 (ceilings 2918400/3283200, CFB=0, and a 4 Hz
         `uclampset -a -M 1024` against every top-app app pid)
  OFF  = op13perf level 0 = stock (rated ceilings, CFB=1, no uclamp lift)

The third-party game is written as "the game"; it is a Unity title. Its trace is
of the TITLE SCREEN, which is a real rendered 3D scene but is not gameplay -- see
section 6.

Synthetic workers run as uid 10999 and are moved into the top-app cpuset so the
observer can see them:
  compute = uninterrupted shell arithmetic loop, 100 % duty
  wake    = same loop in ~4.6 ms bursts behind a 20 ms timed read on a fifo,
            ~38 % duty at ~40 Hz. The timed read is a mksh builtin, so unlike a
            `sleep` loop it adds no fork per cycle.

================================================================================
1. Is a 250 ms window dominated by one or two threads?  Mostly NO
================================================================================
"share" = fraction of all top-app CPU delivered in that window, over every thread
compared, not just the ten reported.

| trace        | active windows | rank1 share | top-2 share | threads >=10% | top-2 >= 80% |
|--------------|----------------|-------------|-------------|---------------|--------------|
| launch       | 33 of 74       | 50 %        | 85 %        | 2             | 52 % of win  |
| scroll  ON   | 45 of 122      | 30 %        | 54 %        | 4             | 24 %         |
| scroll  OFF  | 56 of 123      | 34 %        | 57 %        | 3             | 27 %         |
| switch       | 50 of 105      | 44 %        | 68 %        | 3             | 30 %         |
| game    ON   | 231 of 231     | 23 %        | 39 %        | 3 (>=5%: 7)   | **0 %**      |
| game    OFF  | 231 of 231     | 23 %        | 39 %        | 3 (>=5%: 7)   | **0 %**      |
| wake         | 115 of 115     | 100 %       | 100 %       | 1             | 100 %        |
| compute      | 116 of 116     | 100 %       | 100 %       | 1             | 100 %        |

App launch is the only real workload that looks like the premise: top-2 holds
85 % of the window, and does so in half the windows. Scroll and app-switch are
three-to-four-thread workloads. The game is not close: a median of 21 busy
threads per window, rank1 holding 23 %, and **not one window out of 462 where two
threads held 80 %**.

The 100 % rows are the synthetic workers and are a sanity check on the metric,
not a finding.

2. Is the dominant TID stable, or does it rotate?  It depends on the workload
================================================================================
| trace       | distinct rank1 TIDs | consecutive-window persistence |
|-------------|---------------------|--------------------------------|
| launch      | 11 over 33 windows  | 31 %                           |
| scroll ON   |  9 over 45          | 43 %                           |
| scroll OFF  | 12 over 56          | 24 %                           |
| switch      | 16 over 50          | 20 %                           |
| game ON     |  4 over 231         | **94 %** (224x the same thread)|
| game OFF    |  2 over 231         | **96 %** (226x the same thread)|

A steady-state renderer has one clearly dominant thread and keeps it. Launch and
app-switch rotate through a pool -- Chrome's main thread, NetworkService,
ThreadPoolForeground, VizCompositor, RenderThread -- and the leader changes
between four windows out of five.

Consequence for a controller: a rule that picks "the busiest thread" and pins or
boosts it would be chasing a different thread every window during exactly the
transitions where responsiveness matters most.

================================================================================
4. The controlled pair: duty cycle alone decides the cluster
================================================================================
Same uid, same cgroup, same module state, same shell, same machine, back to back.
The only difference is whether the thread ever sleeps.

| worker            | duty  | prime share | runq_wait median | p90     | max     |
|-------------------|-------|-------------|------------------|---------|---------|
| compute (ON)      | 100 % | **100 %**   | 0.000 ms         | 0.093   | 0.457   |
| wake    (ON)      | ~38 % | **0 %**     | 0.480 ms         | 2.371   | 9.038   |
| wake    (OFF)     | ~38 % | **0 %**     | 0.369 ms         | 2.225   | 6.391   |

A thread that never sleeps gets the prime cluster and never waits. The same
thread, doing 4.6 ms of the same work and then sleeping 20 ms, never reaches the
prime cluster in 230 windows across both module states, and picks up the highest
runqueue wait measured anywhere in this session.

That is the S1 result that matters. The regime DATA.md 42-43 left open is not
limited by a frequency ceiling; in it the dominant thread is not on the cores the
ceiling governs at all.

================================================================================
5. Does the oplus clamp explain the placement?  NOT ESTABLISHED
================================================================================
With the module ON, `uclamp_max != 1024` appears in **0 of 705** reported rank1
rows across every trace. That is the module working as designed -- its 4 Hz
`uclampset -a -M 1024` erases the guard's clamp -- and it means none of the
module-ON traces can say anything about the clamp.

With the module OFF the clamp does appear, but only on the 100 % duty worker, and
the two runs disagree.

  compute OFF, within-run cross-tab (n=116):
      uclamp_max = 1024   n=47   prime 98 %   runq median 0.047 ms
      uclamp_max =  346   n=69   prime  3 %   runq median 0.414 ms
  attribution run, module OFF, same kind of worker (n=84):
      uclamp_max = 1024   n=69   prime 100 %  runq median 0.030 ms
      uclamp_max =  346   n=15   prime 100 %  runq median 0.063 ms

In the first run clamp and loss of the prime cluster co-occur almost perfectly,
with a 9x runqueue wait. In the second the identical clamp value left the thread
on a prime core in every window. The one visible difference: the second run's
thread never migrated at all (0 migrations per window; the first run's moved in
50 % of windows), which is consistent with the V5 finding that the effective
clamp is latched at enqueue -- a thread that is never re-placed keeps the core it
already has, clamp or no clamp.

The A/B/A probe that was supposed to settle this could not: by the time it ran,
phase A was already at 100 % prime, so holding uclamp.max at 1024 for the middle
third had no headroom to show an effect.

**So: association observed once, not reproduced. No S2 arm should be justified on
it.** Settling it needs wake/placement events, not 250 ms sampling.

The wake-heavy worker was **never clamped in either module state** (0 of 230
windows). Whatever keeps it off the prime cluster, it is not the guard.

================================================================================
6. What the traces do not cover
================================================================================
- The game trace is the title screen. Tapping "enter" resumed a 48.5 GB resource
  download (19.4 GB already present) over LTE, so it was force-stopped after
  ~50 s and roughly 0.5 GB. Gameplay is unmeasured, and gameplay is where the
  thermal and thread-count behaviour would actually differ.
- `top-app` on this ROM is not one app. It held up to seven application processes
  at once (plus system_server, filtered by UID), so "dominant thread of the
  foreground app" is really "dominant thread of the top-app cpuset". Narrowing to
  the resumed activity's pid is not implemented.
- System-UID foreground apps are invisible: launching Settings (uid 1000) leaves
  the observer with only residual app-uid processes.
- Scroll ON vs OFF differ in median runq_wait (0.365 vs 0.130 ms) but also in
  which threads led and in page state. Nothing is claimed from that pair.
- Single session, single device, one trace per cell except the synthetic pair.
